STREAM SUITE — QUICK START GUIDE

Made by BabyDreamsy
Stream Suite: Built By Streamers. Powered by Community.

1. WHAT STREAM SUITE IS

Introducing STREAM SUITE, a comprehensive analytics & fun commands system designed to upgrade your streams while also providing a much clearer & more REALISTIC insight of your stream's performance!

Stream Suite is a package of Streamer.bot actions, commands, triggers, queues, and C# scripts made for Twitch streamers.

It records community activity such as attendance, chat messages, follows, raids, Bits, subscriptions, resubscriptions, gifted subscriptions, stream categories, stream-start and stream-end information, and fun-command statistics.

It also creates individual stream summaries and can generate weekly, monthly, and yearly reports from your locally stored stream history.

Stream Suite is designed to become your main system for reviewing community activity from each stream. It does not replace Twitch for official financial information such as advertisement revenue, payouts, or tax-related totals.

Attendance is not the same as a live viewer count. Stream Suite records a person after a supported event identifies them, such as their First Words message or an Attendance Check Channel Point Reward. That person remains on the attendance list for that session even if they leave later. Silent viewers who never activate an observable event may not be recorded.

---

2. WHY STREAM SUITE WAS MADE

I made Stream Suite because Twitch's normal analytics did not always match how my streams actually felt.

A stream could feel active, social, and successful because different people stopped by, chatted, lurked, followed, subscribed, cheered, gifted, or raided at different times. However, the final dashboard could still make the stream look disappointing when most of the attention was placed on average viewers, peak viewers, and similar view-based measurements.

Those numbers can be useful, but they do not tell the entire story of a community.

I wanted a system that could answer simpler and more personal questions:

- Who showed up at any point during the stream?
- Who keeps returning?
- How active was chat?
- Who followed, subscribed, cheered, gifted, or raided?
- How much community activity happened across the full session?
- How many people from one stream or reporting period returned during the next one?

Stream Suite keeps that information locally through Streamer.bot and organizes it around each individual stream. Its purpose is not to create an artificial score. Its purpose is to preserve more of what actually happened.

---

3. HOW STREAM SUITE COMPARES WITH TWITCH AND TRACKING SITES

Twitch's Own Analytics

Twitch's Creator Dashboard and Stream Summary provide official platform information. This includes viewer statistics, chatters, new followers, subscription information, traffic information, revenue, advertisements, and payouts.

Twitch remains the correct source for official platform and financial data.

Stream Suite adds a different view by recording custom attendance, per-user activity, command histories, returning attendees, and locally generated reports according to the streamer's own rules.

Public Twitch Tracking Sites

Public tracking sites generally focus on information such as:

- Average and peak viewers
- Hours watched
- Stream history
- Follower growth
- Channel rankings
- Games and categories
- Broader Twitch trends
- Comparisons between channels

These sites can be useful for seeing public history and outside trends. However, they cannot automatically know a streamer's private Streamer.bot variables, custom attendance rules, excluded-user lists, command activity, or preferred report format.

The Simple Difference

- Twitch Analytics: official platform, viewer, follower, subscription, traffic, revenue, advertisement, and payout information.
- Public tracking sites: public history, rankings, growth, viewership, categories, and broader trends.
- Stream Suite: locally recorded attendance, community activity, Twitch events, fun commands, true attendee-overlap retention, and customizable stream-by-stream reporting.

Use Stream Suite as your main system for community-focused stream analytics. Keep using Twitch for official financial and platform-controlled information, and use public tracking sites whenever outside rankings or broader trends are useful to you.

4. PREREQUISITES

Stream Suite is not a standalone program. It is made for Streamer.bot.

Before installing it, you need:

1. A Windows computer capable of running Streamer.bot.

2. Streamer.bot v1.0.7, the stable version this package was built and checked against.

3. A Twitch account connected to Streamer.bot.

4. An active internet connection for Twitch events and account features.

5. A safe backup of your current Streamer.bot setup.

---

5. STEP-BY-STEP INSTALLATION GUIDE

Step 1 — Install and Open Streamer.bot

Download Streamer.bot v1.0.7 from https://streamer.bot/downloads, extract its ZIP file into a normal local folder, and run Streamer.bot.exe.

DO NOT place Streamer.bot inside a cloud-synchronized folder such as OneDrive, Dropbox, or Google Drive.

Step 2 — Connect Your Twitch Account

In Streamer.bot, open:

Platforms > Twitch > Accounts

Connect your Twitch broadcaster account and confirm that Streamer.bot can connect to Twitch. A separate bot account is optional.

Step 3 — Back Up Your Existing Setup

Before importing Stream Suite, confirm that Streamer.bot has a recent backup.

This protects any personal actions, commands, triggers, queues, and testing versions you already use.

Step 4 — Download and Extract Stream Suite

1. Visit the official Stream Suite download page: https://ko-fi.com/s/58ec72e935
2. Complete the 100% FREE purchase and download process through Ko-fi.
3. Extract the downloaded ZIP somewhere easy to find, such as your Documents folder.
4. Open the `Stream_Suite_4.0_FINAL` folder.

Step 5 — Import the Main Package

1. Open the `Main Package` folder.
2. Locate `Stream_Suite_4.0_Main_Package.sb`.
3. Click **Import** on the Streamer.bot toolbar.
4. Drag the `.sb` file into the **Import String** area.
5. Confirm that the preview title says **STREAM SUITE** and lists 28 actions, 16 commands, and one action queue.
6. Click **Import**.

Step 6 — Review the Required Placeholders

Read the Feature Summary for every action you plan to use.

Replace `streamername` with your lowercase Twitch login wherever instructed. Replace `botname` with your separate bot login or remove that placeholder if you do not use one.

Never paste a real email address, phone gateway, Google App Password, webhook, or private file path into a public copy of Stream Suite.

Step 7 — Create and Connect Channel Point Rewards

Creator-specific reward IDs are intentionally removed from the public release because every Twitch channel has different reward IDs.

Create and connect your own rewards as needed:

- Attendance Check → STREAM SUITE - Attendance Check
- FIRST! → STREAM SUITE - FIRST! Tracker
- Send Streamer a Text → STREAM SUITE - Send Streamer a Text

Attendance Check already includes a First Words trigger, so normal chat participation can record attendance even when the optional reward is not configured.

Step 8 — Review and Enable Commands

Every imported command is disabled by default. Review permissions, cooldowns, and messages before enabling anything.

Fun and community commands:

- `!bite` — BITE!
- `!bonk` — BONK!
- `!hug` — Hugs Tracker
- `!hstats` — Hugs Stats
- `!hboard` — Hugs Leaderboard
- `!pat` — Pats Tracker
- `!pstats` — Pats Stats
- `!pboard` — Pats Leaderboard
- `!fstats` — FIRST! Stats

Report commands:

- `!wreport` — Generate Weekly Report
- `!mreport` — Generate Monthly Report
- `!yreport` — Generate Yearly Report

Administrative reset commands:

- `!resetattendance` — Reset Attendance
- `!resetfirst` — Reset FIRST!
- `!resethugs` — Reset Hugs
- `!resetpats` — Reset Pats

Reset commands should never be available to ordinary viewers.

Reset Variables does not include a public command. Run that action manually or connect it to a private Stream Deck button after reading its Feature Summary.

Step 9 — Review Disabled Actions

The following actions remain disabled until their configuration is reviewed:

- Stream Start Protocols
- Stream End Protocols
- Send Streamer a Text

Do not enable email-to-SMS features until every placeholder, permission, security warning, and carrier limitation has been reviewed.

Step 10 — Test Before Your Next Live Stream

Use a controlled test stream or duplicated Streamer.bot setup.

Test attendance, commands, follows, raids, Bits, subscription events, Stream Start, Stream End, saved reports, and any custom Channel Point Rewards you connected.

Confirm that every Execute C# Code sub-action compiles successfully before relying on the package live.

9. DATA AND SECURITY REMINDERS

Stream Suite stores community activity locally through Streamer.bot variables and generated report files.

Protect files that contain:

- Attendance histories
- Per-user chat activity
- Subscription and Bits information
- Raid and follow records
- Generated stream reports
- Email-to-SMS settings
- Sender addresses or App Passwords

Never publish a configured `.sb` export without inspecting it first. A configured export may contain private credentials or channel-specific information even when the original public template did not.

If a Google App Password is ever exposed, revoke it immediately and create a new one.
